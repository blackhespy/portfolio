Overview

The Business Challenge

Cyclistic is a bike-share program with 5,800+ bicycles across 600 docking stations. The marketing team hypothesized that converting casual riders into annual members would maximize long-term profitability. My task: analyze one month of historical trip data to understand how casual and member riders differ — and recommend data-driven conversion strategies.

This is a capstone case study from the Google Data Analytics Certificate, where I acted as a junior analyst in Cyclistic's marketing analytics team.

Core Question: How do annual members and casual riders use Cyclistic bikes differently — and what does that tell us about how to convert casual riders into members?

Phase 01

Data Preparation

I used Cyclistic's publicly available historical trip data, scoped to one month. The dataset included 13 columns across 258,678 ride records. My first step was understanding the data structure and assessing quality.

  • Removed: Duplicate trip IDs to ensure ride uniqueness
  • Assessed: Missing station data — decided to retain records since station fields weren't essential for the primary analysis
  • Engineered: Two new columns — ride_length (end time minus start time) and day_of_week (from start timestamp)
  • Grouped: Ride lengths into 10-minute intervals for pattern analysis
  • Tools: Excel and Power Query for all transformations

The data columns included: ride ID, bicycle type, start/end timestamps, start/end station names, GPS coordinates, and user type (casual vs. member).

Phase 02

Analysis & Key Findings

Ride distribution and trend analysis across casual vs. member user segments

The 258,678-ride dataset revealed a clear behavioral split between the two user groups:

  • 76% of all rides were taken by members; 24% by casual riders
  • Casual riders had a higher average ride duration (30+ minutes) while members averaged 15-20 minutes
  • 99% of all rides lasted under 60 minutes; 95.3% under 30 minutes
  • 66.5% of rides lasted under 10 minutes — and of those, 78% were members

Peak time and station usage analysis across the full month

Peak Time Analysis

  • Peak hour: 5 PM — accounts for 8.46% of total daily rides for both user types
  • Peak day: Thursday — 13.1% of all weekly rides; heavily dominated by the 10-minute ride category
  • Highest avg ride length by hour: 2 PM at 13.23 minutes per ride
  • Highest avg ride length by day+hour: Tuesday at 2 AM — 21.25 min/ride, with 79% of those being casual riders

Station Analysis

The most popular start and end stations were clustered around areas with high transit connectivity and tourist attractions. Clinton St & Washington Blvd ranked #1 for both starts and ends — located next to a major transit loop and near the Chicago River, making it attractive for both commuters and leisure riders.

Key Insight: Members ride frequently and short — classic commuting behavior. Casual riders ride longer and less often — classic leisure behavior. This is the foundation of the conversion strategy.

Phase 03

Recommendations

  • Create a 30-Minute Pass: Most rides are under 30 minutes. A "10 rides/week, up to 30 min each" membership tier directly targets casual rider behavior at a price point between day-pass and annual.
  • Peak-Hour Promotions: Run targeted campaigns 3–6 PM when usage peaks, specifically promoting membership for commuting use cases.
  • Referral System: Incentivize existing members to invite casual riders. Reward both parties to accelerate network growth.
  • Station Infrastructure: Invest in facilities at the top-traffic stations (near transit and tourist spots) — that's where conversion opportunities are highest.
  • Weekend & Low-Day Passes: Create special pricing for Sunday and Monday (lowest usage days) to build the habit of regular riding among casual users.
Further Opportunity: If Cyclistic can link customer IDs to ride records, behavioral cohort analysis would unlock deeper conversion modeling — identifying which casual riders are most likely to convert and when.
